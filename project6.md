  <h2 style="font-size: 2rem; margin-bottom: 8px;">ME14: Mechanical Transmission & Mechatronic Toy</h2>
  <h3 style="font-size: 1.1rem; color: #666; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 30px;">Caltech ME14 Course Projects</h3>
  
  <p>
    The final leg of course modules in ME14 involved team-based design projects. One of the primary engineering challenges was the Transmission Design and Fabrication Project—a staple of Caltech mechanical engineering giving students hands-on experience with gear train analysis and precision machine shop fabrication.
  </p>

  <figure style="margin: 35px 0; text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://diego-0303.github.io/images/GEARBOX.jpeg" alt="Machined Mechanical Gearbox Transmission" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 4px 16px rgba(0,0,0,0.08);">
    <figcaption style="margin-top: 10px; font-size: 0.9rem; color: #666; font-style: italic;">Figure 1: Custom machined multi-stage gearbox transmission assembly designed to strict dimensional and load tolerances.</figcaption>
  </figure>

  <p>
    Working within tight budget and dimensional constraints, we designed, calculated gear ratios for, and machined a compact transmission. This project applied theoretical stress, fatigue, and gear reduction calculations directly to lathe, mill, and gear-cutting machinery.
  </p>

  <p>
    The second project in ME14 was a Mechatronics Final Project defended in formal Preliminary and Critical Design Reviews (PDR / CDR). My team designed an interactive pet toy combining acrylic enclosures, IR sensors, pop-out actuators, and a ball launcher mechanism.
  </p>

  <figure style="margin: 35px 0; text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://diego-0303.github.io/images/PET_TOY.png" alt="Mechatronic Pet Toy Assembly" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 4px 16px rgba(0,0,0,0.08);">
    <figcaption style="margin-top: 10px; font-size: 0.9rem; color: #666; font-style: italic;">Figure 2: SolidWorks CAD model and acrylic physical prototype of the IR-activated mechatronics pet toy.</figcaption>
  </figure>

  <p>
    This project integrated mechanical CAD modeling in SolidWorks, sensor integration, microcontroller programming, and actuator driving, providing valuable experience in interdisciplinary mechatronics engineering.
  </p>
